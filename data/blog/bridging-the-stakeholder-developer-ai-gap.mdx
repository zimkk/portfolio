# Translating Ambiguity into Code: How Forward Deployed Engineers Bridge the Executive-Dev Divide

If you ask a CEO what they want from AI, they will tell you: *"We want to leverage generative intelligence to streamline our global claims workflow and unlock operational efficiency."*

If you take that sentence and paste it into a Jira ticket for your core engineering team, they will stare at it with deep existential despair.

Engineers need to know:
- What is the exact JSON schema of incoming payloads?
- What are the timeout thresholds on third-party webhook integrations?
- What happens when a customer uploads a password-protected PDF?
- Who approves transactions exceeding $10,000?

The reason 80% of enterprise software initiatives stall is not technical inability. It is **Translation Failure**.

A Forward Deployed Engineer exists specifically to sit directly in the gap between high-level business strategy and low-level code execution.

## The Translation Pipeline

```
[Executive Strategy KPI: "Automate Insurance Claims"]
                       │
                       ▼
    [Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) Embed]
     ├─ Shadow claims adjusters for 3 days
     ├─ Extract 14 unwritten business invariants
     └─ Define concrete OpenAPI schema & error thresholds
                       │
                       ▼
[Core Dev Team Receives Precision Specifications & Working Scaffolding]
```

## The 3 Rules of Tactical Requirement Extraction

1. **Watch the Screen, Do Not Just Listen to the Speech**: When operators tell you how they do their job, they describe the ideal process. When you watch them work, you see the 12 weird manual Excel workarounds they use to fix broken data.
2. **Turn Unwritten Rules into Code Invariants**: Every time an operator says, *"Oh, if the policy was issued before 2021, we always check box B,"* write that immediately as a Python assertion.
3. **Ship a Scaffold in 48 Hours**: Deliver a functional, clickable prototype connected to real staging data within two days. Concrete code accelerates business consensus faster than fifty slide decks.
