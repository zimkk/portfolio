# OpenAI Dots vs Meta Muse for Small Business: The Always-On Agent Era Arrives

For three years, the dominant AI interface was a chat box: you ask, it answers, it waits. On **September 29, 2026**, two of the largest AI companies shipped products built around a different idea: **agents that keep working when you are not talking to them.**

OpenAI launched **Dots** at DevDay 2026 in San Francisco. The same day, Meta expanded its **Muse** agent to small businesses. Here is what each actually does, how they differ, and what builders should take from the design choices.

## OpenAI Dots: Agents With Their Own Computer

According to OpenAI's launch coverage:

- **Model:** Dots run on **GPT-6 Astra**.
- **Runtime:** each Dot gets **its own cloud computer and browser**, isolated unless the user connects it to their accounts.
- **Integrations:** connects to **more than 4,000 apps** through plugins.
- **Channels:** reachable in ChatGPT (web, desktop, mobile), **Slack**, and **Microsoft Teams**, with text messaging announced as coming.
- **Always-on behavior:** Dots can work across multiple projects, send progress updates, ask questions mid-task, and do **"proactive research"** in the background. That proactive mode is restricted to **read-only** access to connected apps.
- **Visibility:** users can watch the Dot's computer while it works instead of waiting for a final answer.

### Controls and Safety

The launch leans heavily on permissioning, which is the right instinct:

- Built-in rules decide what a Dot can do autonomously and what needs approval.
- **Custom Rules** let users "allow, block or require approval for specific actions."
- **Auto-review** applies to actions that affect accounts, and sensitive tasks such as password changes require user approval.

### Availability and Pricing

- **One Dot is included at no extra cost** for ChatGPT **Pro** and **Business Premium** users in eligible markets.
- For Pro, reports list eligible markets as **excluding the European Economic Area, Switzerland, and the UK** at launch.
- **Enterprise, Edu, and Healthcare** workspaces get a **beta** when an admin enables it, with enterprise pilots for specialist Dots.
- OpenAI said Dots conversations do not count toward ChatGPT usage limits, and described options to scale a Dot's speed or monthly work volume. Coverage noted **no published per-Dot price** for extra capacity yet.

## Meta Muse for Small Business: Context Over Autonomy

Meta's Muse agent launched earlier in September in the **US and Canada**. On September 29, Meta expanded it with **Muse for Small Business**.

- **Goal:** help owners run operations and customer communication using context about what the business sells, its brand voice, and its customer inquiries.
- **Integrations:** Shopify, Dropbox, Slack, Asana, Box, Canva, Figma, Granola, HighLevel, Intuit QuickBooks, Klaviyo, Lovable, Notion, Stripe, and Zoom, plus Instagram professional analytics, Facebook Pages, and Meta ad accounts.
- **Tasks cited in coverage:** customer communication, cash flow and inventory processes, and website creation.
- **Pricing:** a free tier with usage limits, with reported paid tiers at **$20 and $100 per month**.

Meta's framing, quoted by TechCrunch: small business owners "told us they're short on hours, not ideas."

## Architecture Lessons for Teams Building Their Own Agents

Both launches validate patterns I have been recommending to clients building internal agents.

### 1. Give agents a sandbox, not your laptop

Dots run in **isolated cloud computers**. That is the correct default: the blast radius of a confused or manipulated agent is limited to its own environment until a human connects more.

**For your build:** run browser and code-execution agents in ephemeral containers or VMs, with network egress controls and no standing credentials.

### 2. Separate "observe" from "act"

OpenAI restricts background **proactive research to read-only** access. Reading is where agents deliver most value with least risk; writing is where incidents happen.

**For your build:** issue read-scoped tokens by default, and require a separate, approval-gated flow to obtain write scopes.

### 3. Make permissions user-legible

"Allow, block, or require approval" is a permission model a non-engineer can understand. That matters because the person approving actions is usually a business user.

**For your build:** express policies as a small set of human-readable rules per tool and action, backed by enforcement at an API gateway, not by prompt instructions.

### 4. Context is the moat for vertical agents

Meta's bet is that an agent knowing your catalog, customers, and brand voice beats a smarter generic agent. For most businesses, that is correct: **retrieval quality and integration depth** decide usefulness more than raw model capability.

### 5. Always-on means always-attacked

An agent that reads email and web pages continuously is continuously exposed to **prompt injection**. Treat every piece of content the agent reads as untrusted, keep authorization outside the model, and log every action with enough detail to reconstruct what happened.

## Key Takeaways

- **OpenAI Dots** (GPT-6 Astra) are always-on agents with their own cloud computer and browser, 4,000+ app integrations, and allow/block/approval controls; one Dot is included with Pro and Business Premium.
- **Meta Muse for Small Business** grounds an agent in business context through integrations like Shopify, Stripe, and QuickBooks, with a free tier and reported $20 and $100 paid tiers.
- The shared design lessons: **sandboxed execution, read-first permissions, user-legible approval rules, and deep context**.
