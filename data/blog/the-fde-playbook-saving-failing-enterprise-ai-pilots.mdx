# The FDE Playbook: How I Rescue Failing $2M Enterprise AI Pilots in 14 Days

Every enterprise AI disaster starts with the same presentation slide: a sleek architectural diagram featuring three frontier LLMs, an abstract vector store, and a box labeled "Autonomous Value Generation."

Six months and $2 million later, the client is staring at a demo that works 70% of the time in staging, crashes on real ERP payloads, and has terrified the compliance team into an indefinite freeze.

Management schedules another alignment meeting. The consulting agency proposes another three-month discovery phase.

As a Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE), my job is simple: **stop having meetings, embed where the operational ambiguity lives, and ship working code directly into production in 14 days.**

Here is the exact diagnostic and execution framework I use to rescue failing enterprise AI pilots.

## Why Enterprise AI Pilots Stall

Enterprise AI projects almost never fail on model capability. They fail on **The Last Mile Friction**:
1. **The Schema Fantasy**: Staging data was clean, syntactically perfect JSON. Production data is a 15-year-old PostgreSQL table with null byte corruptions and mixed date encodings.
2. **The 80% Trap**: A model that is 85% accurate looks miraculous in a slide deck, but in production operations, an 85% accuracy rate means 15 out of every 100 customer transactions are silently corrupted.
3. **The Organizational Chasm**: Executives want "AI transformation," while the frontline operators who actually understand the business rules are terrified the tool will create three times more manual cleanup work.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The First Law of Forward Deployed Engineering**: You cannot solve production ambiguity from an offshore ivory tower. You must sit next to the operational team, watch how they handle edge cases, and encode those unspoken business invariants directly into software.

## The 14-Day Rapid Rescue Protocol

```
[Days 1-3: Forensic Triage] ──► Audit logs, isolate failure modes, compute real baseline accuracy
            │
            ▼
[Days 4-7: Deterministic Containment] ──► Wrap LLMs in hard invariant validation gates & AST checks
            │
            ▼
[Days 8-11: Integration & Operator UX] ──► Build fast inline review UI with sub-second feedback loops
            │
            ▼
[Days 12-14: Production Cutover] ──► Shadow routing, canary deployment, and automated telemetry audit
```

### Phase 1: Days 1 to 3 (Forensic Telemetry and Ground Truth Triage)

On Day 1, I disable all synthetic evaluation benchmarks. We run 500 real historical customer transactions through the failing pipeline and log every single decision point.

We immediately separate failures into three distinct buckets:
- **Data Ingestion Failures**: Malformed inputs, missing headers, or encoding anomalies (usually 40% of all failures).
- **Semantic Hallucinations**: The model making ungrounded assumptions (usually 35%).
- **Business Rule Divergence**: The model producing a logically sound answer that violates an unspoken company policy (usually 25%).

```python:fde_triage_harness.py
from typing import List, Dict, Any
import json

class ProductionTriageHarness:
    def __init__(self, ground_truth_dataset: List[Dict[str, Any]]):
        self.dataset = ground_truth_dataset
        self.error_buckets = {
            "ingestion_corruptions": [],
            "semantic_hallucinations": [],
            "policy_violations": []
        }

    def evaluate_transaction(self, raw_input: dict, pipeline_output: dict, true_label: dict):
        if not raw_input.get("customer_id") or "null" in str(raw_input.get("payload")):
            self.error_buckets["ingestion_corruptions"].append(raw_input)
            return

        if pipeline_output.get("decision") != true_label.get("decision"):
            if pipeline_output.get("inferred_amount") != true_label.get("expected_amount"):
                self.error_buckets["semantic_hallucinations"].append({
                    "input": raw_input, "output": pipeline_output, "expected": true_label
                })
            else:
                self.error_buckets["policy_violations"].append({
                    "input": raw_input, "output": pipeline_output, "expected": true_label
                })
```

### Phase 2: Days 4 to 7 (Deterministic Containment and Invariant Gates)

We replace unconstrained prompt engineering with **Deterministic Invariant Gates**. 

If a financial loan or insurance extraction step returns an output, we do not let it proceed based on LLM confidence. We pass it through a strict Python validation suite. If an invariant fails, we route to a deterministic fallback or an operator review queue:

```python:invariant_containment.py
def process_claim_with_containment(raw_claim: dict, model_client) -> dict:
    sanitized_input = clean_legacy_corruptions(raw_claim)
    draft_decision = model_client.extract_structured(sanitized_input)
    
    assert draft_decision.payout_amount <= sanitized_input["policy_limit"], "INVARIANT: Over-limit payout"
    assert draft_decision.deductible_applied >= 0, "INVARIANT: Negative deductible"
    
    return draft_decision
```

### Phase 3: Days 8 to 11 (Operator-in-the-Loop Workflow)

Instead of aiming for 100% full autonomy (which causes compliance panic), we build an **Operator-in-the-Loop Fast Interface**.

The AI pre-fills 95% of the transaction and highlights the exact 5% ambiguity in bright amber. The human operator presses `Enter` to approve or modifies a single dropdown in 1.5 seconds. Operator productivity triples on Day 9.

### Phase 4: Days 12 to 14 (Canary Cutover and Production Ownership)

We deploy in **Shadow Mode** for 48 hours: every live production request is processed simultaneously by the human team and our fortified AI pipeline.

When our automated telemetry proves that the AI pipeline matches or exceeds human precision across 10,000 live operations with zero invariant violations, we flip the canary switch to 20%, then 50%, then 100%.
